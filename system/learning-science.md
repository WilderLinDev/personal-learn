# Learning Science

Evidence for the rules the skills add to [Teach](teach/SKILL.md). Accessed 2026-11-11.

## Core rules

**Retrieve; don't reread.** Practice testing and distributed practice are the only two techniques rated "high utility" by Dunlosky et al. (2013); rereading and highlighting are low. Meta-analyses: g ≈ 0.70 (Adesope et al., 2017); g ≈ 0.50 in real classrooms, 222 studies (Yang et al., 2021).
[APS](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/which-study-strategies-make-the-grade.html) · [Adesope](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/0034654316689306) · [Yang](https://scholars.hkbu.edu.hk/en/publications/testing-quizzing-boosts-classroom-learning-a-systematic-and-meta-/)

**Space it; too long beats too short.** The best gap grows with the retention goal, and a gap that is too short hurts more than one that is too long (Cepeda et al., 2008).
[PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19076480/)

**Relearn to criterion.** Recall each item correctly once per session, again across spaced sessions (Rawson, Dunlosky & Sciartelli, 2013).
[Review](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10648-023-09809-2.pdf)

**Corrective feedback with the reason.** d ≈ 0.48 across 435 studies; corrective, informative feedback works best, praise least (Wisniewski et al., 2020). High-confidence errors are corrected most readily after feedback (hypercorrection; Butterfield & Metcalfe, 2001).
[Wisniewski](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6987456) · [Hypercorrection](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4084803)

**The learner generates; the AI guides.** Self-explanation: g ≈ 0.55 (Bisra et al., 2018). An answer-giving GPT-4 lowered later test scores by 17%; a hint-only tutor largely avoided this (Bastani et al., 2025, PNAS).
[Bisra](https://summit.sfu.ca/item/34322) · [Bastani](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nas/journl/v122y2025pe2422633122.html)

**Mastery before moving on.** Mastery learning: mean effect ≈ 0.52 across 103 studies (Kulik, Kulik & Bangert-Drowns, 1990); far below Bloom's "2 sigma", which has not replicated.
[Overview](https://nintil.com/bloom-sigma/)

## Situational rules

**Worked examples, then fading.** Complete examples help novices and hinder more knowledgeable learners (expertise reversal; Kalyuga, 2007).
[Overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expertise_reversal_effect)

**Interleave only confusable items.** g ≈ 0.42 overall, strongest for similar categories; blocked practice can win when items differ (Brunmair & Richter, 2019).
[Meta-analysis](https://www.psychologie.uni-wuerzburg.de/fileadmin/06020400/2019/Brunmair_Richter_in_press__2019_META-ANALYSIS_OF_INTERLEAVED_LEARNING.pdf)

## Excluded

- **Pre-questions:** help only the exact questions asked (g ≈ 0.54), barely anything else (g ≈ 0.04) (St. Hilaire et al., 2023).
- **Learning styles:** no adequate evidence (Pashler et al., 2008). [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26162104/)
- **Self-rated mastery:** immediate judgments of learning are poor predictors (Nelson & Dunlosky, 1991); Mastery is derived from spaced recall instead. [Bjork & Bjork](https://bjorklab.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/13/2021/01/RABjorkELBjorkJARMAC2020ForPostingSingleSpaced.pdf)

## Design references

- Review items follow Matuschak's prompt properties: focused, precise, consistent, tractable, effortful. [How to write good prompts](https://andymatuschak.org/prompts/)
- Scheduling is a simple multiplier rule with a full log; FSRS can replace it later. [Benchmark](https://expertium.github.io/Benchmark.html)
