# Knowledge Quality

## Claims
For important claims, identify what is claimed, what evidence supports it, whether the source is primary or secondary, what assumptions or limitations apply, and whether credible interpretations compete.

## Sources
Use the strongest suitable source available. For changing topics, verify freshness. Do not cite a source merely because it supports a preferred conclusion.

## Uncertainty
Use plain descriptions such as well established, supported by current evidence, plausible but uncertain, disputed, or unknown from available evidence. Do not manufacture numerical confidence scores without a domain-specific method.

## Mastery
Ask the learner to explain a concept in their own words, distinguish it from a nearby concept, or apply it to a new example. Repeating a phrase without understanding should not advance mastery.

## Cross-domain connections
A connection should explain a shared mechanism, causal influence, useful analogy, prerequisite, contrast, or application. Avoid links added only because two topics share a keyword.
