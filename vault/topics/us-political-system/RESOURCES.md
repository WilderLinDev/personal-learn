# US Political System Resources

## Knowledge

- [The Constitution: What Does it Say? — National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/constitution/what-does-it-say)
  Official overview of the Constitution's seven articles, the three branches, relations among states, and federal supremacy. Use as the primary constitutional map.
- [Branches of the U.S. Government — USAGov](https://www.usa.gov/branches-of-government)
  Plain-language guide to the legislative, executive and judicial branches and their checks. Use for a quick institutional refresher.
- [The Executive Branch — The White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/government/executive-branch/)
  Current overview of the President, Cabinet, 15 executive departments and federal agencies. Use to check the executive-branch structure.
- [5 U.S.C. § 101 — House Office of the Law Revision Counsel](https://uscode.house.gov/uscode-text?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title5-section101&num=0&edition=prelim)
  The statutory list of the 15 executive departments. Use when checking the department names.
- [The House Explained — U.S. House of Representatives](https://www.house.gov/the-house-explained)
  Explains the House's population-based representation and 435 voting seats. Use for the House/Senate comparison.
- [About the Senate and the Constitution — U.S. Senate](https://www.senate.gov/about/origins-foundations/senate-and-constitution.htm)
  Explains equal state representation and the two-senators-per-state rule. Use for the federalism/representation link.
- [Political Parties — Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/classroom-materials/political-parties/)
  Historical and institutional overview of party organisations, the two major parties and minor parties. Use to avoid treating parties as constitutional branches.
- [2024 Democratic Party Platform](https://democrats.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/2024-Democratic-Party-Platform.pdf)
  Primary-source statement of the Democratic Party's 2024 policy priorities. Use as an example of a party platform, not as a timeless description of every Democrat.
- [2024 Republican Party Platform](https://prod-static.gop.com/media/RNC2024-Platform.pdf)
  Primary-source statement of the Republican Party's 2024 policy priorities. Use alongside the Democratic platform to compare documented positions at that time.
- [Beyond Red vs. Blue: The 2026 Political Typology — Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/politics/2026/06/10/beyond-red-vs-blue-the-political-typology/)
  Survey-based analysis that groups US adults into nine political-value groups. Use to understand variation within and across party coalitions.
- [Americans continue to view both major parties negatively — Pew Research Center, May 1, 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/politics/2026/05/01/americans-continue-to-view-both-the-republican-and-democratic-parties-negatively/)
  National survey of 5,103 US adults, April 20–26, 2026. Use for dated measures of party favourability.
- [Trust in Federal Government Sinks to New Depths — Gallup, October 13, 2026](https://news.gallup.com/poll/715361/trust-federal-government-sinks-new-depths.aspx)
  Annual Governance poll, September 1–17, 2026. Compares trust in federal branches with trust in state and local government.

## Wisdom (Communities)

- No community practice is needed for this introductory institutional topic. Future lessons can use public hearings, election materials and local government meetings as real-world examples.

## Gaps

- A future lesson may need a more detailed, nonpartisan explanation of how federal courts review state and federal laws, using Supreme Court opinions and the Constitution Annotated.
