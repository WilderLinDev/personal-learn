Finance · Lesson 01

A Map of the US Stock Market

The key is to separate where trading happens, what you own, how a market is measured, and where your retirement money is held.

11 November 2026 · A beginner's map of stocks, funds, ETFs, indexes and retirement plans

The core model: five different layers
  1. Market: where buyers and sellers trade.
  2. Asset: what you own, such as a stock or bond.
  3. Fund: a basket that owns assets.
  4. Index: a measurement of a group of assets.
  5. Account: where your money and investments are held, such as a 401(k).
An option is a contract based on an asset. It is not the same thing as owning the asset.

1. Primary market vs. secondary market

In the primary market（一级市场）, an issuer sells newly created securities to investors and receives the money. An initial public offering (IPO，首次公开募股) is one example. Companies can also issue new shares later.

In the secondary market（二级市场）, investors trade securities that already exist. If you buy a listed Apple share from another investor, your money normally goes to that seller, not to Apple.

Primary market

Company → Investor

The company issues new shares and receives the proceeds.

Secondary market

Investor ↔ Investor

Investors trade existing shares. The seller receives the money.

Why it matters

Investors can buy and sell more easily. This creates liquidity（流动性）and helps the market discover prices.

The secondary market does not usually send money to the company for each trade. It still helps companies because investors are more willing to buy new shares when they know they may be able to sell later.

2. Stock, fund and ETF: what is the relationship?

A stock（股票） represents part ownership of one company. If you own a share of a company, you own a small part of that business. A share may give you voting rights and a right to dividends if the company declares them.

A fund（基金） pools money from many investors and uses it to buy a portfolio（投资组合）of assets. A stock fund may own shares in dozens or hundreds of companies. A bond fund owns bonds. A fund is a basket, not one company.

An ETF（交易所交易基金） is a type of fund. It pools investors' money and owns a portfolio of assets. You buy and sell ETF shares on a stock exchange during the trading day, much like you trade a company stock.

ThingWhat you ownSimple example
One stockA small ownership interest in one companyOne share of Apple
Mutual fundA share of a pooled investment portfolio; orders are normally priced once each trading dayA mutual fund holding many US stocks
ETFA share of a pooled portfolio; trades on an exchange during the dayAn ETF holding many US stocks

Important: Not every ETF tracks an index. Some ETFs are actively managed. An ETF describes how the fund is traded and structured, not necessarily how its investments are selected.

What is an index fund?

An index fund（指数基金） is a fund that tries to follow a market index. It can be an ETF or a mutual fund. For example, an S&P 500 index fund tries to follow the S&P 500.

An index（指数） is only a measurement. You cannot buy the index itself. You buy a fund that tries to track it.

Remember: An ETF is a type of fund. An index fund is a fund strategy. An index is a measurement. One ETF can be an index fund, but not every ETF is an index fund.

3. Nasdaq, S&P 500 and the Dow

This part is confusing because people use the word “Nasdaq” in two different ways.

Nasdaq Stock Market is a stock exchange（证券交易所）: a trading venue where buyers and sellers trade listed securities. The New York Stock Exchange (NYSE) is another major US exchange.

Nasdaq indexes are measurements. The most common ones are:

NameWhat it measuresHow to think about it
Nasdaq CompositeThousands of stocks listed on the Nasdaq Stock MarketA broad view of Nasdaq-listed companies; it has a strong technology presence.
Nasdaq-100About 100 of the largest non-financial companies listed on NasdaqMore concentrated in large growth and technology-related companies.
S&P 500500 leading large US companiesA broad measure of large US companies; it covers about 80% of available US market capitalisation.
Dow Jones Industrial Average (the Dow)30 large, established US companiesA famous but much smaller index. It is price-weighted.

Price-weighted（价格加权） means that a company's share price affects the Dow's weight. It does not simply give each company the same weight, and it does not weight companies by their total market value.

You may be mixing up two names: the Dow Jones Industrial Average and the S&P 500. They are different indexes. The Dow has 30 companies; the S&P 500 has 500.

When a news headline says “the Nasdaq fell”, it often means the Nasdaq Composite index, not that the exchange itself fell.

4. How is the stock market connected to US retirement?

The most useful idea is that a retirement plan is often an account or plan, while stocks and funds are the investments inside it.

401(k): a retirement account through work

A 401(k)（美国雇主退休储蓄计划） is a defined-contribution plan（缴费确定型计划）. An employee can send part of each paycheck into the account. The employer may add matching money. The employee often chooses investments from a list offered by the plan, such as mutual funds or target-date funds.

The account's value depends on contributions, investment gains or losses, and fees. A 401(k) is not itself a stock or an ETF. It is the account that holds investments.

Traditional pension: a promised benefit

A traditional pension is usually a defined-benefit plan（待遇确定型养老金）. It promises a retirement benefit based on a formula, often using salary and years of service. The plan invests its assets, but the worker's promised benefit is calculated by the plan's rules rather than being simply the value of a personal stock portfolio.

Social Security: a separate public programme

Social Security（美国社会保障） is a public social-insurance programme. It is funded mainly by payroll taxes. It is not a personal account where your own payroll taxes are invested in Apple or an S&P 500 ETF.

How the pieces connect: A worker contributes money to a 401(k) → the account offers investment choices → the worker chooses an S&P 500 index fund → that fund owns shares in companies → those shares trade in the stock market. If the shares rise or fall, the fund and the retirement account can also rise or fall.

Many retirement plans invest in stocks and bonds, so market performance affects retirement savings. But not every retirement benefit is directly linked to an individual's stock-market balance: a traditional pension and Social Security work differently.

5. One complete example

Imagine an employee puts $200 from each paycheck into a 401(k) and chooses an S&P 500 index mutual fund.

  • 401(k): the retirement plan/account.
  • Mutual fund: the investment vehicle.
  • S&P 500: the index the fund tries to follow.
  • Company shares: the underlying assets held by the fund.
  • Stock exchanges: places where those shares can be traded.

The employee does not directly buy “the S&P 500”. The employee buys fund shares in a retirement account.

Check your understanding

Someone says: “I hold an S&P 500 ETF in my 401(k).”
