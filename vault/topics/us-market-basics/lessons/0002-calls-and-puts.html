Calls and Puts: Options in Plain English

A stock is ownership. An option is a contract that gives someone a right, or creates an obligation, to trade an asset at a set price.

11 November 2026 · The basic meaning of “my calls” and “my puts”

The core model: A call gives its buyer the right to buy. A put gives its buyer the right to sell. Both have a strike price and an expiration date. The buyer pays a premium for this right.

1. What is an option?

An option（期权） is a contract. It gives the buyer the right, but not the obligation, to buy or sell an underlying asset（标的资产） at a fixed price, on or before a specified date, according to the contract's rules.

The underlying asset can be a stock, an ETF or a stock index. So options are related to stocks, but an option is not a share of stock. If you buy a call option, you do not become a shareholder just because you own the option.

2. Call vs. put

OptionBuyer has the right to…Common market view
Call option（看涨期权）Buy the underlying asset at the strike price.Usually bullish: the buyer expects the price to rise.
Put option（看跌期权）Sell the underlying asset at the strike price.Usually bearish or protective: the buyer expects a fall or wants insurance.

The buyer has a right. The seller, also called the writer（期权卖方）, has an obligation if the buyer exercises the option. Selling an option is therefore a different risk position from buying one.

3. Four important parts of an option

  • Underlying asset: the stock, ETF or index the option is based on.
  • Strike price（行权价）: the fixed price at which the buyer can buy or sell.
  • Expiration date（到期日）: the last date when the option can be used under its contract rules.
  • Premium（权利金）: the price the buyer pays for the option.

A standard US stock option contract generally represents 100 shares. For example, if the quoted premium is $2 per share, one standard contract usually costs $200 ($2 × 100). Check the contract details: adjusted contracts and options on indexes may have different specifications.

4. A simple call example

Imagine a stock is trading at $100. You buy one call option with:

  • Strike price: $105
  • Premium: $2 per share
  • Contract size: 100 shares
  • Total premium paid: $200

The break-even price at expiration is $107: the $105 strike plus the $2 premium. The stock can rise and you can still lose money if it does not rise enough by the expiration date.

5. A simple put example

Now imagine the stock is trading at $100. You buy one put option with a $95 strike and a $2 premium. You pay $200 for one standard 100-share contract.

  • If the stock falls to $80, the put lets you sell at $95. That is $15 per share of value, or $1,500. After the $200 premium, your profit is $1,300 before fees.
  • If the stock is $95 or higher at expiration, the put has no exercise value. You lose the $200 premium.

The break-even price at expiration is $93: the $95 strike minus the $2 premium.

6. What do people mean by “my calls” or “my puts”?

In a trading conversation:

  • “I bought calls” or “I have calls” usually means the speaker owns call option contracts.
  • “I bought puts” or “I have puts” usually means the speaker owns put option contracts.
  • “My call” or “my put” can refer to one of those positions. But in ordinary English, “it's my call” also means “it's my decision”, so context matters.

People often use “calls” to express a bullish view and “puts” to express a bearish view. But a call or put is a contract, not a guarantee that the trader will make money.

7. Why options are risky

Options have a limited life. Their value depends on the underlying price, the strike price, time left and market volatility（波动率）. The right direction is not enough: the price may move too slowly, or not far enough, before expiration.

  • Buyer: if an option expires without value, the buyer can lose the entire premium.
  • Seller: some short-option positions can lose much more than the premium received. Certain uncovered calls have theoretically unlimited loss.
  • Time: as expiration gets closer, the time value of an option often falls, all else being equal.
Safety rule: Buying a stock and buying a call option are not the same. A stock has no expiration date. An option does. Before trading an option, understand its strike, expiration, premium, contract size and maximum possible loss.
