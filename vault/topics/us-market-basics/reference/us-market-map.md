## Market structure

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Primary market | New securities are issued; the issuer receives the proceeds. |
| Secondary market | Investors trade existing securities with one another. |
| Stock exchange | A regulated trading venue, such as Nasdaq or NYSE. |

## Investment objects

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Stock | A share of ownership in one company. |
| Fund | A pooled portfolio that invests in assets such as stocks or bonds. |
| ETF | A fund whose shares trade on an exchange during the trading day. |
| Index | A measurement of a selected group of securities; you cannot buy the index directly. |
| Index fund | A mutual fund or ETF designed to follow an index. |

## Four widely followed US indexes

| Index | What it follows |
|---|---|
| Nasdaq Composite | Thousands of stocks listed on Nasdaq. |
| Nasdaq-100 | About 100 of the largest non-financial Nasdaq-listed companies. |
| S&P 500 | 500 leading large US companies; about 80% of available US market capitalisation. |
| Dow Jones Industrial Average | 30 large US companies; price-weighted. |

:::note
Nasdaq is both an exchange name and part of several index names. The Dow is not the S&P 500.
:::

## Retirement

| Plan | Basic structure |
|---|---|
| 401(k) | Defined-contribution workplace plan. Contributions go into an account; investments determine much of the final balance. |
| Traditional pension | Defined-benefit plan. Retirement income is promised through a formula, often based on salary and service. |
| Social Security | Public social-insurance programme funded mainly by payroll taxes, not a personal stock-market account. |

:::key[Remember]
A retirement account is the container. A fund is an investment inside it. The index is the benchmark the fund may follow.
:::

## Options

| Term | Buyer has the right to… |
|---|---|
| Call | Buy the underlying asset at the strike price. |
| Put | Sell the underlying asset at the strike price. |
| Strike | The fixed price written in the contract. |
| Expiration | The date when the option ends. |
| Premium | The price paid by the option buyer. |

:::note
A standard US stock option generally represents 100 shares. Buyers can lose the full premium. Some option sellers can lose much more.
:::
