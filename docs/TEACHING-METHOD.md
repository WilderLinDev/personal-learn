# Teaching Method

This document records the teaching principles integrated into the Personal Learn skill. It is designed to be usable without invoking a separate Teach plugin.

## 1. Three dimensions of learning

Deep learning combines:

- **Knowledge:** accurate understanding built from high-quality, high-trust resources.
- **Skills:** the ability to retrieve, reason with, and apply knowledge through practice and feedback.
- **Wisdom:** judgment developed through real-world use and interaction with practitioners or communities.

The right balance depends on the subject. A theoretical subject may be knowledge-heavy; a practical discipline may be skill-heavy.

## 2. Mission and zone of proximal development

Every lesson should connect to the learner's mission. Use learning records to estimate the learner's current level and choose the next step that is challenging but achievable.

The mission is already documented in MISSION.md. Do not repeatedly ask the learner to restate it. If the user wants to change it, confirm before editing the mission.

## 3. Lesson design

A lesson is the primary teaching unit:
- One tightly scoped objective.
- One tangible outcome.
- Short enough to complete in a focused sitting.
- Only the knowledge required for the outcome.
- Concrete examples, mechanisms, and useful contrasts.
- Retrieval practice or application with feedback.
- A high-quality source for further exploration.
- Links to related lessons and references.

Prefer a series of small, connected lessons over one long lecture. Keep cognitive load manageable.

## 4. Knowledge versus skill instruction

For knowledge acquisition, reduce unnecessary difficulty so working memory can focus on understanding. Explain the mental model first, then use examples and counterexamples.

For skill acquisition, use effortful practice: demonstrate, let the learner try, provide immediate feedback, and gradually increase difficulty.

Interleave related skills when it improves transfer. Do not mix unrelated topics for its own sake.

## 5. Fluency and storage strength

In-session ease is not the same as long-term retention.

- **Fluency strength:** ease of answering now.
- **Storage strength:** ability to retrieve knowledge after time has passed.

Build storage strength with retrieval practice, spacing, desirable difficulty, and application. Use the review policy and update it based on demonstrated recall.

## 6. Evidence and sources

Before teaching substantive or consequential topics, find suitable high-quality sources. Prefer primary sources where appropriate, then authoritative secondary sources and high-quality explanations.

Cite claims in lessons. Record important recurring sources in RESOURCES.md. State uncertainty and limitations honestly.

## 7. Reference materials

A lesson is for guided learning; a reference is for later recall.

Create compact reference pages when useful for:
- Definitions and glossaries.
- Formulas, syntax, and algorithms.
- Procedures and checklists.
- Diagrams, sequences, and decision rules.

Keep terminology consistent across lessons. Reuse shared assets rather than duplicating components.

## 8. Wisdom and practice beyond the lesson

When real-world judgment or practical feedback is central, help the learner find credible practitioners, communities, classes, or real situations in which to test the knowledge. This is optional and topic-dependent.

## 9. Source of truth and continuity

The GitHub repository stores the durable mission, learner context, lessons, references, knowledge, and learning history. New conversations should retrieve the relevant subset before teaching. A new chat must not mean starting from zero.
